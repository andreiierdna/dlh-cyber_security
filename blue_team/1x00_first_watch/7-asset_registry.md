## Reconciliation Notes

### 1. Assets Found in the Network Scan but Not in Existing Documentation

**10.10.2.99 / UNKNOWN-01** is the clearest Central shadow-IT finding. It is a Linux 4.x system on the server subnet with SSH and web services on ports 8888 and 9090, but it has no DNS hostname, documented owner, approved function or corresponding entry in the previous asset inventory. Because it is actively connected to a sensitive server segment while its purpose and management authority are unknown, it is classified as **Shadow IT (unmanaged)** rather than merely Unknown. 

**10.10.10.200 / Westside unknown Linux device** is a second shadow-IT system. It exposes SSH, HTTP and port 3000, and Sarah Park explicitly states that it is absent from IT documentation and may have been installed unofficially as a monitoring or web-based tool. Its presence is particularly significant because Westside already relies on a consumer router and lacks a dedicated firewall, so an unmanaged host adds another uncontrolled access and software-maintenance point. 

**WS-WC-XRAY at 10.10.10.100** is also new at the individual-asset level. The onboarding material documents that Westside provides X-ray services, but it does not identify a dedicated X-ray workstation. The scan therefore converts a previously known clinical capability into a specific network asset that should now be formally assigned an owner, vendor, support status and software baseline.

**NAS-01** was described indirectly in backup documentation and the network diagram, but it was not part of the original formal Central server list. The scan now confirms a specific managed device at `10.10.2.41` running Synology DSM 7. It should therefore be promoted into the authoritative registry as a distinct **Data Store**, rather than remaining merely a backup destination reference. 

The scan also provides specific identities for assets that were previously documented only as categories, including **12 UniFi APs, individual patient monitors, infusion pumps, two nurse-call endpoints and three HID badge readers**. This is an inventory improvement rather than evidence of unauthorized equipment because the corresponding asset classes were already documented.  

### 2. Assets Mentioned in Documentation but Not Appearing in the Network Scan

The **GE Revolution CT scanner** is documented at Central but does not appear under an identifiable hostname in the scan. The absence should not be treated as proof that the scanner has been decommissioned because the scan summary explicitly states that devices powered off at the time may not have responded. The correct registry status is therefore **Unknown** pending physical verification and collection of its network identity. 

The **possible second Westside server** mentioned by Marcus also does not have a clearly matching scan result. One possibility is that `10.10.10.200` is the previously rumored server; however, the evidence does not establish that relationship, and Sarah herself could not identify the scanned device. The two records should therefore remain separate until physical inspection or switch-port tracing proves whether they refer to the same asset. 

The documented **physician iPads** do not appear as a recognizable inventory population in the scan. This may result from intermittent wireless connectivity, unidentified hostnames, guest/wireless segmentation not represented in the summary, or the devices simply being offline during the scan. Because approximately 25 iPads are documented but MDM status is unclear, the inventory remains incomplete until serial numbers, assigned users and management enrollment can be reconciled. 

The prior incident's **intern personal laptop** also cannot be matched to a current scan entry. This is expected if the device was removed after the incident, but there is no evidence in the supplied material confirming disposition. The registry therefore preserves the asset as historical **Shadow IT** instead of assuming it was formally decommissioned.

Several network and physical assets—including the **FortiGate 100F, Cisco core switch, Cisco access switches, Central UPS, CCTV/DVR systems and physical server/network rooms**—are absent from the Nmap host list. This is not inherently contradictory because some have no routable management address, may not have been included in the simplified scan output, or are non-networked physical infrastructure. Their existence is independently documented by the network diagram, controls evidence and physical assessment. 

### 3. Discrepancies and Contradictions Between Sources

**Central network segmentation is described using different terminology but the sources ultimately agree on the security condition.** The scan groups endpoints into `10.10.1.0/24`, servers into `10.10.2.0/24` and medical devices into `10.10.3.0/24`, which could superficially suggest segmentation. However, the scan explicitly states that these are addressing conventions only and that no VLAN or firewall separation exists. It further confirms that Sarah's HQ workstation could reach all subnets without restriction. This corroborates Marcus's earlier statement that the effective environment is one flat `10.10.0.0/16` network.  

**print-srv-01 was previously marked unverified but is now confirmed active.** The original asset record had not been physically validated for more than a year, creating uncertainty about whether the server still existed. The network scan resolves that uncertainty by detecting `print-srv-01` at `10.10.2.31` running Windows Server 2012 R2 and offering SMB and printing services. 

**Endpoint population counts are close but not identical.** The existing inventory estimated approximately 320 Central Windows workstations, approximately 45 Westside workstations, approximately 120 HQ workstations and approximately 30 HQ laptops.  The scan reports roughly 290 additional Central workstations plus the explicitly listed Central endpoints, around 36 identified Westside workstations, approximately 120 HQ workstations and approximately 25 HQ laptops. The differences are consistent with stale inventory data and devices being offline rather than demonstrating definite loss or unauthorized removal.

**The Central thin-client count remains unresolved.** Documentation estimates approximately 60 clinical thin clients, while the scan summary identifies only four named ER thin clients. Because the scan was response-based and abbreviated, the 56-device difference requires a targeted inventory check before assets are marked decommissioned.

**Medical-device counts broadly reconcile but remain approximate.** Documentation records approximately 80 Philips patient monitors and approximately 120 BD Alaris infusion pumps. The scan identifies named examples plus approximately 65 additional monitors and 110 additional pumps. This broadly supports the documented fleet sizes, but a device-by-device biomedical inventory is still required because the scan output intentionally omits many individual entries. 

**The MRI identity is now materially clearer.** Earlier documentation referred to one Siemens MAGNETOM MRI running Windows XP but did not provide an IP address or workstation hostname. The scan identifies `WS-RAD-01`, `10.10.1.70`, as the MRI control workstation and confirms Windows XP SP3. This should become the canonical technical identifier for the legacy MRI control asset. 

**The billing server's support condition is more severe than the earlier inventory conveyed.** Earlier records described Ubuntu 18.04 as approaching or reaching end-of-life. The scan states that standard support ended in June 2023 and that Extended Security Maintenance is available but not activated. More importantly, incident evidence confirms that the server has already suffered ransomware and subsequent cryptomining compromise, so the registry must reflect both the support deficiency and demonstrated compromise history rather than treating the issue as theoretical.  

**The network diagram's DMZ designation for web-srv-01 requires configuration validation.** Earlier documentation places `web-srv-01` in a DMZ, while the scan assigns it an address in the `10.10.2.0/24` Central server addressing range. This is not sufficient to prove that the DMZ is incorrectly implemented because IP addressing alone does not establish firewall-zone membership; however, it is a material discrepancy that should be verified against the FortiGate interface and VLAN configuration. The network diagram itself was explicitly described as simplified and incomplete. 

**The original asset documentation was demonstrably incomplete.** It previously identified incomplete endpoint counts, an unconfirmed Westside server, an unrecorded core-switch model, undocumented Westside wireless equipment, unclear iPad management and an incomplete cloud-service inventory.  The new scan resolves several individual identities but also adds two completely undocumented Linux systems, confirming that MedDefense still does not have a fully reconciled source of truth.

### Registry Conclusion

The consolidated registry identifies **56 asset records**, substantially exceeding the minimum requirement of 20 and covering servers, endpoints, network devices, medical IoT, data stores, applications and physical infrastructure. The highest-priority reconciliation actions are to identify and authorize or remove the two shadow-IT Linux systems, physically verify the CT scanner and possible second Westside server, reconcile the incomplete thin-client and mobile-device populations, and validate the actual DMZ/network-segmentation configuration. These actions are justified because the scan confirms that the entire routed internal environment is currently reachable without meaningful segmentation, meaning an unidentified or unmanaged asset is not isolated to its nominal subnet and may expose systems elsewhere in MedDefense.
