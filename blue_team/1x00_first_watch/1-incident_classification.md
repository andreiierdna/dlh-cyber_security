## Incident Classification Table

The classifications below apply the CIA Triad to the six incidents in Marcus Webb’s log. The broader onboarding material confirms that MedDefense operates a largely flat internal network, has weaknesses in backup design, and lacks a formal incident response and business continuity program, which increases the operational significance of several incidents.  

| Incident                                                         | Primary CIA Pillar  | One-Sentence Justification                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Secondary Pillar             | Secondary Impact Explanation                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Ransomware on billing-srv-01**                             | **Availability**    | Availability was the primary impact because ransomware encrypted the billing server and prevented the finance team from processing insurance claims for four days.                                                                                                          | **Integrity**                | Integrity was also affected because the ransomware made unauthorized changes to the server’s data by encrypting it, while the three-week-old backup increased the risk that recent billing information could not be restored accurately.      |
| **B — Patient portal broken access control**                     | **Confidentiality** | Confidentiality was the primary impact because authenticated patients could access other patients’ laboratory results by changing a URL parameter, exposing information to unauthorized individuals.                                                                        | **None clearly established** | No secondary CIA impact is demonstrated by the incident description because the vulnerability enabled unauthorized viewing, but there is no evidence that records were altered or that the portal became unavailable.                         |
| **C — Incorrect pharmacy dosages**                               | **Integrity**       | Integrity was the primary impact because a faulty database update script overwrote medication dosage values and caused the pharmacy system to display incorrect clinical information across all three sites.                                                                | **Availability**             | Availability was indirectly affected because, although the system remained online, unreliable dosage information reduced its practical usability for safe medication management until the error was identified and corrected.                 |
| **D — Public website defacement**                                | **Integrity**       | Integrity was the primary impact because an unauthorized party modified the public website and replaced the legitimate homepage with a political message.                                                                                                                   | **Availability**             | Availability was also affected because the legitimate website content was unavailable to users until the site was restored from backup approximately two hours later.                                                                         |
| **E — Nine-hour EHR outage**                                     | **Availability**    | Availability was the primary impact because the EHR system was inaccessible for nine hours during the migration, forcing physicians to use paper records instead of the electronic system.                                                                                  | **None clearly established** | No secondary CIA impact is confirmed because the incident description identifies service interruption but does not state that EHR data was disclosed or modified without authorization.                                                       |
| **F — Intern laptop on internal network running torrent client** | **Confidentiality** | Confidentiality is the primary concern because an unmanaged personal device operated for three weeks on the internal network and could reach the same network segment as the HR file share, creating a pathway for unauthorized exposure of sensitive employee information. | **Integrity**                | Integrity is also at risk because a potentially compromised or untrusted endpoint on the internal network could be used to alter shared files or other accessible systems, although no actual modification is documented in the incident log. |

### Classification Rationale

The incidents demonstrate that the CIA Triad is useful because the same event can affect more than one security objective. Incidents **A** and **E** are primarily availability failures because business or clinical systems could not be used when required. Incidents **C** and **D** are primarily integrity failures because trusted information or system content was changed without authorization or without correct control. Incident **B** is a direct confidentiality breach because unauthorized users could view protected patient information. Incident **F** is best classified as a confidentiality exposure because the untrusted device had network proximity to the HR file share, but the available evidence supports a **risk of compromise rather than a confirmed data breach**. This distinction is important because professional incident classification should separate demonstrated impact from plausible impact.
