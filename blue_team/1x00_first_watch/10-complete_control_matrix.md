# MedDefense Health Systems — Complete Control Matrix

**Prepared for:** James Chen, Deputy CISO
**Purpose:** Authoritative consolidated inventory of security controls identified across the assessment.

## Rating Method

Effectiveness reflects **current implementation**, not theoretical design quality:

* **Strong** — properly configured for its intended scope, actively operating, and without a material evidenced implementation weakness.
* **Adequate** — operating and useful, but limited by scope, coverage, configuration, testing, or maintenance gaps.
* **Weak** — materially bypassable, substantially incomplete, or documented/designed but not evidenced as implemented.

For Part 2, average effectiveness uses **Strong = 3, Adequate = 2, Weak = 1**. Averages of **2.50–3.00 = Strong; 1.50–2.49 = Adequate; below 1.50 = Weak**.

Task 8 establishes the five highest-priority assets as the EHR, PACS/MRI environment, Active Directory, network core, and medical IoT fleet.

### Registry conclusion

The authoritative inventory contains **27 controls**. The increase from the original 16 is not evidence that MedDefense deployed eleven new safeguards during the assessment. Several controls were discovered later—such as HID access, local audit logs, RAID resilience, UPS continuity and intersite VPNs—while six functional entries arise from the Task 6 MRI strategy, which remains largely **design-state rather than operational protection**. The distinction is critical because the Board should not interpret proposed compensating controls as already reducing current exposure.

### Interpretation

The matrix remains heavily weighted toward **technical preventive controls**. This concentration does not establish mature defense-in-depth because several technical controls have restricted scope and the MRI controls contributing to the matrix are not yet implemented.

The most significant structural omissions are **Administrative × Detective, Administrative × Corrective, Physical × Corrective and Physical × Compensating**. The original gap analysis already established the absence of formal administrative incident detection and recovery processes, while no physical mechanism equivalent to alternate-site recovery, comprehensive environmental recovery or documented infrastructure restoration is evidenced.  

The low **1.50** detective score is also material. MedDefense produces firewall, SSH, Windows, Linux, Apache and EHR audit records, but these records are primarily local and manually reviewed; there is no routine centralized correlation or alerting. A control that records an event but does not reliably bring the event to an analyst's attention provides evidence after compromise but limited capability to stop attacker dwell time.

## Overall Coverage Judgment

**No Top 5 critical asset qualifies as Well-Protected.** Two are **Partially Protected** and three are **Under-Protected**.

The principal issue is not the complete absence of controls. MedDefense has meaningful perimeter filtering, authentication hardening on selected systems, workstation antivirus, backups, logging and physical-security mechanisms. The weakness is that these controls do not consistently align with the organization’s most critical assets. The EHR receives comparatively strong host-level protection but its database remains broadly reachable; PACS has no evidenced backup; the MRI and medical IoT estate lack implemented segmentation; Active Directory lacks MFA and centralized detection; and network infrastructure can be physically reached through inadequately controlled areas. Central's underlying architecture remains effectively flat despite addressing conventions that separate workstations, servers and medical devices. 

For Board reporting, the defensible summary is therefore: **MedDefense has 27 identified controls, but control quantity overstates current protection because six entries associated with the MRI are proposed rather than operational, major detective and recovery functions remain absent, and three of the five most critical asset groups remain under-protected.**
