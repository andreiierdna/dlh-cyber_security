## Top 5 Most Critical Assets

### 1. EHR System — ehr-srv-01 and ehr-db-01

The **EHR system ranks first** because all three CIA dimensions can translate directly into patient-care consequences. Loss of availability has already demonstrated operational impact: a prior nine-hour outage forced clinicians to use paper records instead of the electronic system.  Integrity is equally important because incorrect clinical records, medication information, allergies, or treatment history could result in unsafe decisions, while a confidentiality breach would expose PHI and create regulatory and legal obligations. The combination of clinical dependency, sensitive data, and demonstrated outage impact makes the EHR MedDefense's most critical information asset.

### 2. PACS and MRI Imaging Environment

The **PACS/MRI environment ranks second** because diagnostic imaging directly supports diagnosis and treatment decisions, while the MRI processes approximately 45 studies each day and cannot be disconnected from PACS without disrupting patient care. A PACS or MRI availability failure would delay imaging-dependent diagnosis and could create treatment delays; an integrity failure is more severe because altered or mismatched images could cause clinicians to act on incorrect diagnostic evidence. The environment also contains a known structural vulnerability: the MRI workstation runs Windows XP and cannot be patched or upgraded because changing the certified operating system would affect medical-device certification. The combination of direct clinical dependency and an unavoidable legacy-security condition places the imaging environment immediately below the EHR in priority.

### 3. Active Directory — ad-dc-01 and ad-dc-02

The **Active Directory environment ranks third** because it is an enterprise-wide dependency rather than an isolated application. The domain controllers provide authentication and directory services for MedDefense systems, meaning compromise could give an attacker a mechanism to create accounts, alter privileges, reset credentials, or gain persistent access across multiple departments. Availability loss could also prevent legitimate users from authenticating to clinical and business services. Unlike compromise of a single workstation, compromise of Active Directory can transform one security incident into organization-wide loss of trust in identities and access rights.

### 4. Network Core — FortiGate 100F and Core Switching Infrastructure

The **network core ranks fourth** because nearly every MedDefense service depends on it for connectivity, and the current architecture magnifies the consequences of compromise. Central workstations, servers, and medical devices are effectively connected through a flat environment, while Westside and HQ depend on VPN connectivity through the single FortiGate 100F.  A configuration failure or successful attack against the firewall, core switch, or VPN infrastructure could therefore isolate entire sites, bypass security restrictions, redirect traffic, or facilitate lateral movement between administrative and clinical assets. The network does not hold the same primary patient record as the EHR, but it is a common dependency whose failure can make multiple critical systems unavailable simultaneously.

### 5. Medical IoT Fleet — Patient Monitors, Infusion Pumps and Nurse-Call Systems

The **medical IoT fleet ranks fifth** because integrity or availability compromise can have immediate patient-safety implications at the bedside. MedDefense operates approximately 80 patient monitors and approximately 120 network-connected BD Alaris infusion pumps, together with IP-based nurse-call infrastructure.  If an attacker alters monitoring information, interferes with device configuration, or makes devices unavailable, clinical staff may receive inaccurate information or lose access to equipment required for ongoing treatment. The risk is materially increased by the current network design: medical devices remain broadly reachable from other internal systems instead of being isolated behind enforceable segmentation controls.
