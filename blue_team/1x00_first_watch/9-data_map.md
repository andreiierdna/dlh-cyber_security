# MedDefense Health Systems — Data Map

The classification below applies the four required sensitivity levels to data documented in the MedDefense environment. **Restricted** is used where unauthorized disclosure could expose protected health information, credentials, financial information, or similarly sensitive personal information and create severe operational or regulatory consequences. **Confidential** is used for sensitive internal business information whose disclosure would cause significant organizational or employee harm. Where a safeguard such as encryption is not identified in the supplied evidence, it is described as **not evidenced** rather than assumed to be absent.

## Data Risk Summary

MedDefense's **most significant data protection weakness is the protection of Restricted patient medical records while they are in use and accessible across the internal environment**, because the organization combines its highest-sensitivity data with both a demonstrated endpoint-control failure and excessive network exposure. The EHR contains PHI and clinically consequential information, yet an unattended nurse-station workstation remained authenticated with a patient record visible for at least 15 minutes, giving any nearby unauthorized person immediate access without having to steal credentials, bypass the firewall or exploit the application. This in-use weakness is amplified by the architecture surrounding the same data: ehr-db-01 is reachable from the broader internal network instead of being restricted to ehr-srv-01, and Central operates a flat environment in which workstations, servers and medical devices lack effective segmentation. The gap is therefore wider than a single workstation issue. A Restricted record is insufficiently isolated **at rest**, broadly reachable **in transit**, and demonstrably exposed **in use**. Because the EHR supports clinical care and PHI confidentiality simultaneously, compromise can produce regulatory exposure, unauthorized record modification and patient-care consequences; the earlier criticality assessment accordingly identifies the EHR as MedDefense's highest-priority information asset.
