# 2. Top 3 Priority Ranking

The Top 3 ranking below is **not based on likelihood alone**. Each position considers both:

* **Likelihood:** How probable is the actor to affect MedDefense given sector intelligence and MedDefense's actual profile?
* **Impact:** If successful, how severely could the actor affect MedDefense's Critical assets, Restricted data, clinical operations, recovery capability, finances and patient safety?

## Rank 1 — Ransomware Groups (Organized Crime)

### Likelihood: **Critical**

Ransomware has the strongest evidence of probable targeting. MedDefense is a 350-bed regional hospital that directly matches the Task 0 preferred ransomware victim profile. It holds valuable Restricted PHI, depends on Critical clinical systems, operates legacy technology, has constrained security resources and is located within a regional peer group that has recently experienced ransomware attacks. MedDefense has also already experienced ransomware and later unauthorized malware on `billing-srv-01`.

### Impact: **Critical**

A successful ransomware intrusion could affect several of MedDefense's Top Five Critical Assets simultaneously. **GAP-007** can enable Active Directory takeover, **GAP-006** gives unnecessary network proximity to the EHR database, **GAP-004** exposes backup and production data to the same destructive event, and **GAP-016** increases the chance the attacker moves undetected. The attacker could therefore steal Restricted PHI, compromise AD, encrypt EHR and other reachable Windows systems, attack backups and create a prolonged clinical outage.

The impact is not theoretical. The Task 0 comparable hospital case involved VPN exploitation, lateral movement across a flat network, Domain Controller compromise, 42 GB of patient-data exfiltration, enterprise ransomware deployment, 23 encrypted servers, approximately 400 encrypted workstations, ambulance diversions and cancelled procedures. Its post-incident weaknesses—unpatched VPN, flat network, reachable NAS backups, no SIEM and no formal incident-response plan—closely match MedDefense's documented posture.

### Ranking Justification

**Critical likelihood + Critical impact = Rank 1.**

No other actor combines such strong evidence of likely MedDefense targeting with an attack model that aligns so precisely with the organization's current gaps.

## Rank 2 — Insider (Negligent)

### Likelihood: **High**

Negligent insider activity is not hypothetical at MedDefense. The previous insider assessment documented a **shared Radiology account**, an **unmanaged Cardiology NAS containing patient information**, and **unsafe handling of an Active Directory administrator credential**. The Task 0 intelligence also identifies negligent behavior as the more common portion of healthcare insider incidents.

MedDefense's clinical environment makes this likelihood especially important because employees require legitimate access to sensitive systems to perform patient-care functions. The organization therefore cannot simply eliminate access; it must control and monitor how that access is used.

### Impact: **High to Critical**

A negligent action can directly affect Critical systems. Shared `raduser` credentials weaken accountability within **PACS/MRI (#2)**. The personal NAS under **GAP-005** places patient information on an unmanaged device attached to a broadly connected internal network. Mishandled privileged credentials can expose **Active Directory (#3)**, which in turn provides access across the enterprise.

The impact may also become indirect: a negligent employee may create the foothold later exploited by an external attacker. An unmanaged device, disclosed password or exposed privileged credential can transform a human error into an enterprise compromise because MedDefense lacks sufficient internal segmentation and centralized monitoring.

### Ranking Justification

**High likelihood + High-to-Critical impact = Rank 2.**

Negligent insiders rank above malicious insiders because they occur more frequently at MedDefense and are already demonstrated, while still having the ability to expose Critical assets and Restricted data.

## Rank 3 — Insider (Malicious)

### Likelihood: **Medium**

The sector evidence supports malicious insider activity, but it occurs less frequently than negligent insider behavior. MedDefense nevertheless has conditions that make malicious activity credible: broad legitimate access to Restricted information, incomplete user offboarding, weak centralized behavioral monitoring and insufficient DLP.

The Task 3 contractor scenario demonstrates a particularly important weakness: an account remained active for 47 days after authorization ended and was subsequently used during off-hours.

### Impact: **High to Critical**

A malicious insider can begin from inside the trusted environment. A clinical user may intentionally access and disclose Restricted EHR PHI, as demonstrated by the Task 3 scenario involving unauthorized access to a politician's record. A privileged IT insider could cause substantially greater damage by targeting Active Directory, network infrastructure or backup systems.

T1 demonstrates this capability through a former administrator who created an unauthorized VPN account and disabled database backups before termination, then used that access to delete production data. The attack required limited external resources because legitimate knowledge and privileged access supplied the necessary capability.

MedDefense's **GAP-013, GAP-014, GAP-016 and GAP-007** make this threat more dangerous by allowing stale accounts, insufficiently monitored valid access, weak bulk-data controls and password-based authentication.

### Ranking Justification

**Medium likelihood + High-to-Critical impact = Rank 3.**

Malicious insiders rank below negligent insiders because deliberate misuse is less likely, but above opportunistic attackers because a malicious insider can intentionally target MedDefense's most valuable assets and recovery mechanisms using trusted access and organizational knowledge.
