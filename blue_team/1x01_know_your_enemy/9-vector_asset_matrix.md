## 5. Threat-Exposure Conclusion

The matrix shows that MedDefense's highest risk does not arise from eight independent attack vectors; it arises from the **interaction between initial-access vectors and the same small set of architectural weaknesses** already identified in the prior assessment.

The dominant exposure pattern is:

**Initial access through phishing, VPN compromise, vulnerable software, insider access, or physical access → internal foothold → flat network → broadly reachable critical services → insufficient segmentation and identity enforcement → compromise of a Critical asset.**

This finding directly reinforces the Prioritized Gap Analysis. Internal segmentation and medical-device isolation are the highest-concentration missing preventive controls, while MFA, stronger network-core protection, centralized detection, and isolated recovery mechanisms are required to prevent one successful attack vector from becoming a multi-asset incident.

The most dangerous MedDefense intersection is therefore not a single vulnerability in isolation: it is **a successful entry vector combined with unrestricted lateral reachability to Critical clinical and infrastructure assets**. The network scan demonstrates that this condition currently exists, including direct network reachability between ordinary endpoints, servers, EHR database services, backup management interfaces, and medical devices.
