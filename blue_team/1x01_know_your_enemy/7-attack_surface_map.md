### External-Surface Boundary

No additional server detected by the **internal** Network Scan Summary can defensibly be classified as Internet-facing solely because it exposes an internal service. For example, `web-srv-01` shows SSH `22` internally, but C-002 establishes that WAN inbound access is restricted to HTTP/HTTPS. Likewise, the undocumented Linux hosts at `10.10.2.99` and `10.10.10.200` are serious **internal** attack-surface findings, but the supplied evidence does not establish direct Internet exposure.

# Section 2 — Internal Surface

## Flat-Network Finding

The defining condition of MedDefense's internal attack surface is the **absence of effective network segmentation**. During the scan, Sarah Park's workstation at Corporate HQ (`10.10.20.10`) could reach every scanned subnet without access restriction. The scanner concluded that a device on one subnet can reach devices on any other subnet. The nominal `10.10.1.0/24`, `10.10.2.0/24` and `10.10.3.0/24` divisions therefore function primarily as addressing conventions rather than enforceable security boundaries.

This finding changes the significance of every internal exposure below. An open management or database service is not limited to a trusted application segment; a compromised workstation, branch endpoint, unmanaged device or insider can obtain network reachability to the service and attempt authentication or exploitation.

### Credential Exposure: PACS and Medical IoT

The supplied 1x00 evidence requires a distinction between **confirmed credential weaknesses** and assumptions. The Complete Control Matrix confirms that **Radiology uses a shared PACS credential**, which weakens individual accountability and increases the impact of credential disclosure. It does **not**, in the evidence provided for this task, establish that the PACS credential is a vendor-default password.

Likewise, the Network Scan and Gap Analysis establish that medical-device HTTP/HTTPS management interfaces are broadly reachable and that C-009 applies only **where applicable**, but the supplied evidence does not conclusively identify a specific factory-default username/password still active on the Philips or BD Alaris fleets. The defensible finding is therefore: **medical IoT authentication is insufficiently evidenced and the management plane is excessively reachable; confirmed use of vendor-default credentials should be validated rather than asserted without evidence.**

# Surface Assessment Summary

**The internal attack surface represents MedDefense's greatest current risk.** The external and human surfaces provide credible initial-access opportunities—particularly the Internet-facing patient portal, O365 identities, VPN-connected sites and phishing of staff—but the internal architecture determines how damaging one successful foothold can become. The Network Scan Summary proves that the routed MedDefense environment is effectively flat: Sarah Park's HQ workstation could reach all scanned subnets without access restrictions. From a single compromised internal endpoint, an attacker obtains network reachability to `ehr-db-01:5432`, `billing-srv-01:3306`, NAS management on `5000/5001`, Active Directory services, RDP-enabled workstations, unsupported Windows XP and Server 2012 R2 systems, and HTTP/HTTPS management interfaces across the medical-device fleet. This directly intersects with the prior Criticality Matrix and Gap Analysis: EHR and Active Directory are only Partially Protected, while PACS/MRI, the network core and medical IoT are Under-Protected; PACS has no evidenced recovery capability, medical IoT lacks segmentation/monitoring/recovery, backups share the production failure domain, and centralized detection remains deficient. The result is that an otherwise limited compromise of a user, branch endpoint or unmanaged system can become an enterprise-wide lateral-movement event affecting Restricted PHI, authentication infrastructure, diagnostic imaging, backup systems and bedside medical technology. **Internal segmentation and containment are therefore the most important attack-surface reduction priority for MedDefense today.**
